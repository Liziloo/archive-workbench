# Archive Workbench

## Purpose

Archive Workbench (AW) is intended to expedite the processing of archival materials by one untrained archivist in preparation for archival storage and publication through Omeka S.

AW exists to make it possible for one person to process an overwhelming volume of museum-grade artifacts without reducing the quality or care given to those materials. Its purpose is to **streamline and reduce human effort wherever possible**, not to lower the standard of archival work.

## Human Judgment and Automation

AW should delegate work to the computer whenever a computer can perform it reliably without creating danger to the archive or its eventual publication.

Routine, deterministic, and administrative work should therefore be automated wherever practical, including tasks such as data entry, database management, process flow, repetitive processing, and tagging.

Questions of historical interpretation, and actions that could have lasting consequences for physical archival artifacts, must remain under human supervision and approval.

AI may be used where it can meaningfully improve the work, but AI-assisted interpretation is collaborative rather than autonomous. AI may propose, assist, organize, or identify possibilities; the human archivist retains responsibility for decisions involving interpretation or consequential changes.

The guiding principle is:

> **If a computer can do it reliably, with no danger to the archive or its publication, it should.**

## Scope

AW is a workbench for processing and preparing archival materials. It is not intended to replace the systems responsible for long-term preservation or public archival publication.

AW should make the path from physical archival material to properly processed, documented, stored, and publishable material as efficient and safe as possible.
